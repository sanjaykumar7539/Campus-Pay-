# ADR-0002: Adoption of a Modular Monolith Architecture

**ADR ID:** ADR-0002  
**Title:** Adoption of a Modular Monolith as the Initial Application Architecture  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 27 July 2026  
**Decision Makers:** CampusPay Engineering Team

---

# Context

CampusPay is a commercial, multi-tenant Campus Financial Operating System designed to support financial transactions, digital identity, attendance, merchant operations, reporting, and administrative services for educational institutions.

As established in ADR-0001, the platform is intended to serve multiple independent institutions while maintaining a single configurable product.

The engineering team must now determine the most appropriate application architecture for the initial development phase.

The primary architectural options considered were:

- Traditional Layered Monolith
- Modular Monolith
- Microservices

This decision affects:

- Code organisation
- Development workflow
- Deployment
- Testing
- Scalability
- Maintainability
- Operational complexity
- Future evolution of the platform

---

# Decision

CampusPay shall initially be implemented as a **Modular Monolith**.

The application will be deployed as a single executable application while being internally organised into well-defined business modules.

Each module shall encapsulate its own:

- Business logic
- Domain models
- Services
- Validation
- Interfaces

Modules shall communicate through clearly defined internal interfaces rather than directly accessing one another's implementation details.

The architecture shall preserve clear module boundaries to enable future extraction into independent services if operational requirements justify such a transition.

---

# Rationale

The Modular Monolith architecture provides the best balance between engineering simplicity and long-term scalability.

It enables rapid product development while maintaining a clean internal structure that supports future growth.

This approach avoids unnecessary operational complexity during the early stages of product development while preserving architectural flexibility.

---

# Architectural Principles

The following principles shall govern the application architecture.

## Business Modules

The system shall be organised around business capabilities rather than technical layers.

Examples include:

- Authentication
- Institutions
- Students
- Faculty
- Wallets
- Payments
- Merchants
- Attendance
- Notifications
- Reports
- Devices
- Audit

Each module represents a cohesive business domain.

---

## High Cohesion

Responsibilities belonging to a business capability shall remain within the corresponding module.

Business rules should not be duplicated across multiple modules.

---

## Low Coupling

Modules should minimise dependencies on one another.

Communication between modules should occur through clearly defined interfaces.

Implementation details shall remain internal to each module.

---

## Single Deployable Unit

During the initial phases of the project, CampusPay shall be deployed as a single application.

This simplifies:

- Deployment
- Debugging
- Local development
- Testing
- Operational management

---

## Independent Evolution

Although deployed together, modules shall be designed so they can evolve independently.

If future operational requirements demand it, a module may be extracted into an independent service with minimal disruption.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Alternative 1 — Traditional Layered Monolith

A conventional application organised primarily into controllers, services, repositories, and models.

### Advantages

- Simple structure
- Familiar to many developers
- Fast initial implementation

### Disadvantages

- Business logic tends to become tightly coupled.
- Module boundaries become unclear as the project grows.
- Difficult to evolve into independently deployable components.

**Decision:** Rejected

---

## Alternative 2 — Microservices

A distributed architecture where each business capability is implemented as an independent service.

### Advantages

- Independent deployment
- Independent scaling
- Technology flexibility
- Team autonomy

### Disadvantages

- Increased operational complexity
- Distributed transactions
- Network communication overhead
- Service discovery requirements
- API gateway requirements
- More complex testing strategy
- Greater infrastructure costs

At the current stage of the project, these trade-offs are not justified.

**Decision:** Rejected

---

## Alternative 3 — Modular Monolith

A single deployable application organised into independently maintained business modules.

### Advantages

- Clear separation of business domains
- Lower operational complexity
- Faster development
- Easier debugging
- Strong transactional consistency
- Simplified deployment
- Clear migration path toward distributed services

### Disadvantages

- Requires disciplined architectural governance
- Module boundaries must be actively maintained
- Developers must avoid creating hidden dependencies

**Decision:** Accepted

---

# Architectural Implications

This decision establishes the following engineering rules.

## Module Ownership

Each business capability shall own its:

- Domain model
- Business rules
- Validation logic
- Internal services
- Internal data access

Other modules shall not directly manipulate these internal components.

---

## Interface-Based Communication

Modules shall expose well-defined interfaces.

Cross-module interactions should occur through these interfaces rather than direct implementation access.

---

## Shared Infrastructure

Infrastructure concerns may be shared where appropriate, including:

- Logging
- Configuration
- Security
- Database connectivity
- Monitoring
- Audit infrastructure

Shared infrastructure shall not contain business logic.

---

## Transaction Management

Where business operations span multiple modules, transactions shall be managed within the monolithic application boundary.

This enables strong consistency without requiring distributed transaction mechanisms.

---

## Future Service Extraction

The architecture shall preserve the option of extracting individual modules into independent services if justified by future operational requirements.

Examples may include:

- Payments
- Notifications
- Analytics

Such extraction shall not require fundamental redesign of the remaining application.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Faster product development
- Simplified deployment
- Lower infrastructure cost
- Easier debugging
- Strong transactional consistency
- Clear module ownership
- Easier onboarding of new developers
- Migration path toward service-oriented architecture

---

## Negative

- Requires disciplined architectural governance
- Risk of accidental coupling if module boundaries are ignored
- Independent scaling of modules is not possible while deployed as a monolith

These trade-offs are considered acceptable for the current stage of CampusPay.

---

# Compliance Requirements

All future development shall comply with the following principles.

- Business logic shall remain within its owning module.
- Direct access to another module's internal implementation is prohibited.
- Cross-module communication shall occur through defined interfaces.
- Shared infrastructure shall not contain business-specific behaviour.
- New modules shall follow the same architectural principles established by this ADR.

---

# Relationship to Other ADRs

This ADR extends:

- ADR-0001 — Adoption of a Multi-Tenant SaaS Architecture

Future ADRs concerning authentication, APIs, database design, and deployment shall conform to the architectural direction established by this document.

---

# Review Schedule

This ADR shall be reviewed if:

- Team size increases significantly.
- Independent scaling of specific business capabilities becomes necessary.
- Operational complexity justifies migration to distributed services.

Until such conditions exist, the Modular Monolith architecture remains the authoritative architectural approach.
